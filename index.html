The method

One delivery, four frames.

This is how we read a video, in the order the body moves. Proximal to distal: the big joints first, the hand last. Each frame has one thing we measure and one thing we fix.

  1. FIG. 01

    Load

    Hips go back first. The back knee stays behind the toes; the hinge stores energy in the posterior chain before anything moves down the mound.

    We look at the hip hinge at peak leg lift

  2. FIG. 02

    Foot strike

    At foot strike the pelvis has already opened toward the plate while the shoulders are still closed. That gap is the primary velocity driver. The pelvis leads, the shoulders wait.

    We look at stride length and trunk position at foot strike

  3. FIG. 03

    Arm path

    A smooth arm path is a sequence, not a shape. Scap retraction leads, then elbow, wrist, fingers. Get the order right and the path takes care of itself.

    We look at the order the arm unfolds in: scap, elbow, then hand

  4. FIG. 04

    Release

    The front leg plants and blocks. Rotation stops there and the energy has nowhere to go but up the chain and out of the hand, well out in front. A consistent release point starts in the legs.

    We look at front knee extension and trunk tilt at release
